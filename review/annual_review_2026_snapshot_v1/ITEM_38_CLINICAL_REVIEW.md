## Verified Clinical Reviewer Notes (Clinical Sources Only)

### 1. Comprehensive Abortion Care Clinical Guidance
- **Primary Clinical Sources:**
  - *Comprehensive Abortion Care: Training and Service Delivery Guidelines for Healthcare Providers*, Maternal Health Division, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), Government of India.
  - *Abortion Care Guideline*, World Health Organization (WHO), Geneva, 2022.
- **Core Clinical Principles:**
  - **Early Assessment:** Timely clinical determination of pregnancy duration (gestational age) ensures the selection of safe, evidence-based care modalities appropriate to the clinical setting.
  - **Dignified Counselling:** Care must be patient-centered, empathetic, and confidential, ensuring individuals understand available options and post-procedure recovery expectations without stigma or delays.
  - **Exclusion of Public Dosages & Mechanics:** In accordance with public health communication standards, specific pharmaceutical dosages and surgical procedural steps are omitted to prevent self-prescription risks and maintain focus on professional facility-based access.

### 2. Emergency Contraception Mechanisms & Method Availability
- **Primary Clinical Sources:**
  - *Family Planning: A Global Handbook for Providers*, WHO / Johns Hopkins, 2022 Edition.
  - *Reference Manuals for Contraception*, Family Planning Division, MoHFW, Government of India.
- **Mechanism of Action:**
  - Emergency contraceptive pills (ECPs) prevent or delay ovulation; they do not disrupt or terminate an established pregnancy (WHO *Abortion Care Guideline*, 2022; WHO *Emergency Contraception Factsheet*).
- **Method-Specific Timeframes:**
  - **Levonorgestrel (LNG) ECP:** Most effective when taken as soon as possible after unprotected intercourse (licensed within 72 hours; evidence supports diminishing efficacy up to 120 hours).
  - **Ulipristal Acetate (UPA) ECP:** Effective up to 120 hours (5 days) where approved/available.
  - **Copper Intrauterine Device (Cu-IUCD):** Highly effective when inserted by a trained clinical provider within 120 hours (5 days) of unprotected intercourse.
- **Facility Availability:** Barrier methods and oral pills are widely accessible through community health workers and primary clinics; IUCDs, injectables, and surgical contraception depend on facility infrastructure and trained clinical personnel.

### 3. Post-Abortion Follow-Up & Clinical Safety
- **Primary Clinical Sources:**
  - WHO *Abortion Care Guideline* (2022) & MoHFW CAC Guidelines.
- **Follow-Up Principles:**
  - Routine mandatory in-person clinic visits are not strictly required following uncomplicated medical or surgical abortion if complete expulsion/termination is clinically assessed or verified via self-assessment protocols and the patient is asymptomatic.
  - In-person assessment and clinical advice must remain accessible, and any individually advised follow-up must be respected.
- **Emergency Red Flag Criteria:**
  - Severe hemorrhage: Soaking $\ge 2$ large sanitary pads per hour for two consecutive hours.
  - Critical red flags requiring urgent evaluation without waiting for a bleeding threshold: Syncope / fainting (`બેભાન થવું`), marked postural weakness, severe or worsening pelvic pain, temperature $\ge 38^\circ\text{C}$ / chills, or purulent foul-smelling vaginal discharge.
- **Voluntary Contraception:**
  - Ovulation can resume as early as 8–10 days post-abortion. Contraceptive counselling is provided voluntarily, with method selection and initiation timing tailored to individual preference, recovery, and clinical eligibility.
